The energy model and both ledgers are defined in `radio-and-energy-model.md`.

All tables below are generated by `analysis/make_supplementary.py` from the files named in each section. Energy values are means over all generated tasks, including tasks that miss their deadline.

## Energy per deadline-satisfying task

Energy per deadline-satisfying task divides the modelled system ledger by the number of tasks meeting their deadlines, i.e. system energy per generated task divided by deadline satisfaction. For the full-day sample:

- DQN-Full: 2.74 J;
- Local: 2.98 J;
- Greedy: 3.00 J;
- DQN-NoV2V: 3.30 J;
- Random: 8.89 J.

The value is computed for each training seed and then averaged; the ratio of the means gives the same values to two decimals. This metric supplements rather than replaces energy per generated task and deadline satisfaction: every generated task consumes energy whether or not it meets its deadline, and a lower value can arise from higher service as well as from lower energy.

## Analytical break-even conditions

This section derives when offloading a task costs less modelled system energy than executing it locally, `E_comm + E_remote < E_local`, and the corresponding condition for the requester ledger.

### Assumptions

1. The calculation uses the mean task of each class (`experimental-configuration.md`): cycles `C` and input size `S` at their class means; the result size is `ρS` with `ρ = 0.05`.
2. Computation energy is `C × e`, with `e_V = 1.0 nJ/cycle` on every vehicle (requester or peer) and `e_R = 0.15 nJ/cycle` on RSUs. It does not depend on the processor's clock rate or core count.
3. A transfer of duration `t` costs `t × P_tx` at the transmitter and `t × P_rx` at the receiver, with `P_tx,V = 0.4 W`, `P_rx,V = 0.8 W`, `P_tx,R = 2.0 W` and `P_rx,R = 2.0 W`.
4. Transfer time is payload bits divided by the link rate. The rate depends only on distance (no fading, shadowing, interference or contention) and follows the CQI mapping of `net/nrv2x_lite.py`. The upload and result-return distances are taken as equal.
5. Only task-attributable energy is counted; queueing changes when a task runs but not its energy, and idle, baseline and cooling power are excluded.
6. The 2 Mbit/s rate floor of the model is never active, because the lowest rate the model produces is 3.0 Mbit/s. The 5 s transfer cap applies to the mean infotainment input only below 4.8 Mbit/s (1.3 and 0.5 Mbit/s for the automated-driving and safety means), so it does not affect the system-ledger thresholds; where it applies, it shortens the counted transfer and can only make offloading appear cheaper.

### Derivation

Write `t_up = 8S / r_up` and `t_down = 8ρS / r_down`, with rates in bit/s.

**Local execution.** `E_local = e_V C`.

**V2V.** The peer computes at the same energy per cycle as the requester, so

`E_sys,V2V = e_V C + (P_tx,V + P_rx,V)(t_up + t_down) = E_local + 1.2 W × (t_up + t_down) > E_local.`

V2V therefore adds communication energy without reducing computation energy, and never lowers the modelled system ledger.

**RSU.**

`E_sys,RSU = e_R C + (P_tx,V + P_rx,R) t_up + (P_tx,R + P_rx,V) t_down = e_R C + 2.4 W × t_up + 2.8 W × t_down.`

Offloading to the RSU lowers system energy when

`2.4 × 8S / r_up + 2.8 × 8ρS / r_down < (e_V − e_R) C = 0.85 nJ × C.`

Ignoring the result return gives the lower bound `r_up > 19.2 S / (0.85 nJ × C)`. In the radio model the RSU downlink rate is at least the uplink rate at every distance (80 MHz and 2.0 W against 20 MHz and 0.4 W), so the return term adds at most a factor `1 + 2.8ρ / 2.4 = 1.058` to the threshold.

**Requester ledger.** For either offloading target the requester pays only for transmitting the input and receiving the result:

`E_user,offload = P_tx,V t_up + P_rx,V t_down < E_local  ⇔  0.4 × 8S / r_up + 0.8 × 8ρS / r_down < e_V C,`

which gives `r_up > 3.2 S / (1 nJ × C)`, increased by at most a factor 1.1 by the return term.

### Thresholds for the mean task classes

| Class (mean task) | Cycles (M) | Input (KB) | E_local (J) | System ledger: RSU uplink rate above (Mbit/s) | System ledger: break-even distance to RSU (m) | Requester ledger: RSU uplink rate above (Mbit/s) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Safety | 600 | 300 | 0.60 | 11.3–12.0 | 306 | 1.6–1.8 |
| Automated driving | 1,200 | 800 | 1.20 | 15.1–15.9 | 270 | 2.1–2.3 |
| Infotainment | 3,600 | 3,000 | 3.60 | 18.8–19.9 | 238 | 2.7–2.9 |

Lowest uplink rate the radio model produces at any distance: 3.0 Mbit/s. The RSU downlink rate is at least the uplink rate at every distance from 1 to 2,000 m.

The break-even distance is the largest vehicle-to-RSU distance at which RSU offloading of the mean task costs less system energy than local execution, computed with the model's own link function (`one_nr_sidelink_hop`). Because the radio model maps signal-to-noise ratio to discrete CQI levels, rate and distance thresholds are step-wise.

Three consequences follow under the reported model:

- Peer and requester vehicles use the same energy per CPU cycle, so V2V adds communication energy without reducing computation energy.
- RSUs use less computation energy per cycle but require sufficiently fast transfers to offset communication energy: about 11–20 Mbit/s for the mean task classes, which the model reaches within roughly 240–310 m of the RSU. Half of the 610 m RSU spacing is about 305 m, so between adjacent RSUs the nearest RSU can lie beyond the break-even distance of the automated-driving and infotainment means.
- The requester-ledger threshold (1.6–2.9 Mbit/s) lies below the lowest rate the model produces (3.0 Mbit/s). A requester-only ledger therefore favours offloading the mean task of every class at every distance, which is why it rewards offloading even when system energy increases.

These thresholds describe the mean task only. Individual tasks vary in cycles and input size (`experimental-configuration.md`), so their thresholds vary accordingly.
